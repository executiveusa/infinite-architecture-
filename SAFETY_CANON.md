# INFINITE ARCHITECTURE — SAFETY CANON

## Core Principle

**Infinite Architecture publishes free construction knowledge. That knowledge must not cause harm.**

Every guide, build system, field note, and tutorial must be evaluated against this safety canon
before publication. The Safety Reviewer agent enforces this canon.

---

## Non-Negotiable Flags

The following conditions ALWAYS require a local engineering and code review disclaimer:

1. **Inhabited structures** — Any structure where humans will sleep, live, or spend extended time.
2. **Load-bearing elements** — Columns, beams, foundations, structural walls, anything carrying loads.
3. **Spanning over people** — Roofs, pergolas, trellises, awnings, any overhead spanning element.
4. **Retaining structures** — Retaining walls holding soil, slopes, or water pressure.
5. **Roofs of any kind** — Barrel roofs, flat roofs, shell roofs, pergola roofs. All require engineering note.
6. **Elevated platforms** — Decks, lofts, elevated floors, anything humans stand on above grade.

### Required Disclaimer Text

> ⚠️ **Engineering Note**: This system involves [element]. Any inhabited structure, load-bearing element, spanning roof, or elevated platform must be reviewed by a licensed local engineer and must comply with local building codes. Infinite Architecture provides open knowledge — not engineering certification. Always obtain proper permits and inspections.

## Material Hazards

### Foam (XPS / EPS / FOAMULAR)
- **Combustible**: Foam must always be fully encapsulated with cementitious or non-combustible material.
- **Never leave exposed foam** in finished structures.
- **Fire spread risk**: Burning foam produces toxic smoke. Full cement coat is mandatory.
- **UV degradation**: Uncoated foam degrades in sunlight. Always coat.

### Cement and Mortar
- **Alkaline**: Fresh cement is caustic. Wear gloves, eye protection.
- **Damp curing**: Cement requires damp curing for 7–28 days. Skipping curing causes cracking and weakness.
- **Mix ratios matter**: Wrong ratios produce weak, crumbly, or cracked shells.

### Mesh and Reinforcement
- **Ordinary fiberglass ≠ alkali-resistant (AR) mesh**: Standard fiberglass cloth degrades rapidly in cement. Use AR-grade mesh only for primary structural reinforcement.
- **Boat fiberglass cloth**: May be used as a catch layer or surface detail layer, but must NOT be the sole cement reinforcement.
- **Diamond mesh and cattle panel**: Good for ferrocement but mesh opening size affects mortar penetration. Match mesh to mix.

### Sealers and Adhesives
- **Pegapiso / porcelanato adhesive**: Use as thin bond coat only. It is NOT a bulk structural material.
- **SikaLatex**: Follow manufacturer dilution ratios. Overuse can trap moisture.
- **Waterproofing coatings**: Apply in correct sequence (cement base → waterproofing → finish). Do not reverse.

## Structural Risk Categories

| Risk Level | Description | Required Action |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| LOW | Decorative elements, garden beds, non-load-bearing planters | Safety note only |
| MEDIUM | Countertops, sinks, outdoor kitchens, freestanding walls | Material warnings + cure protocol |
| HIGH | Roofs, spanning structures, load-bearing walls, inhabited structures | Engineer disclaimer + local permit note |
| CRITICAL | Retaining walls over 1m, elevated floors, bridges | Block publication until engineer review |

---

## Editorial Safety Rules

1. All HIGH and CRITICAL risk build systems must have the Engineering Note at the top of the page.
2. All guides must include material hazard warnings relevant to the materials used.
3. No guide may claim structural performance without field test data.
4. Difficulty rating must be accurate — do not label HIGH risk systems as BEGINNER.
5. Substitution guides must flag which substitutions have NOT been field-tested.
6. The Safety Reviewer has veto power on all content before publication.

## Local Context: Puerto Vallarta, Jalisco, Mexico

- High humidity coastal environment — waterproofing is non-negotiable.
- Seismic zone — any load-bearing or structural element must reference local seismic requirements.
- CONAVI and municipal building codes apply — all inhabited structures require permits.
- Local material names vary — always include Spanish equivalents.
- Rainy season (June–October) affects curing — timing notes required.
