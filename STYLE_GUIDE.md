# INFINITE ARCHITECTURE — STYLE GUIDE

**Status:** Locked for the Awwwards biophilic redesign branch  
**Branch:** `feature/awwwards-biophilic-resize`  
**Project role:** Commercial design-coordination studio, public architecture journal, and 3D concept platform  
**Primary market:** Puerto Vallarta and the surrounding Bay of Banderas region  
**Primary customer:** Airbnb owners, boutique hospitality operators, glamping founders, landowners, real-estate agents, and people planning off-grid projects

---

## 1. Brand position

Infinite Architecture helps people turn land, listings, and rough ideas into memorable places to stay and live.

The studio specializes in:

- biophilic Airbnb and boutique-hospitality concepts
- glamping sites and nature retreats
- domes, vaults, hyper-roof structures, and curved shelters
- off-grid water, solar, shade, food-growing, and ventilation concepts
- 3D visualization for land, listings, and pre-construction sales
- project coordination across designers, engineers, fabricators, suppliers, and local labor
- planting, landscape, and living-system integration

Infinite Architecture is not presented as a licensed architecture, structural-engineering, MEP-engineering, or general-contracting firm unless those licensed services are supplied by named partners. The studio sells **concept design, visualization, planning, procurement support, and project coordination**. Permit drawings, structural calculations, regulated trades, and construction sign-off must be completed by qualified local professionals.

### Brand promise

> **Build places people remember.**

### Commercial statement

> Biophilic concept design and project coordination for Airbnbs, glamping retreats, and off-grid living in Puerto Vallarta.

### Public-benefit statement

> We document better ways to build: healthier materials, climate-aware forms, living air, water intelligence, passive comfort, repairable systems, and structures designed to belong to their landscape.

## 3. Audience hierarchy

### Primary commercial audiences

1. Airbnb and vacation-rental owners who need a distinctive property or expansion.
2. Glamping and retreat founders building small hospitality destinations.
3. Landowners who need a concept before committing to construction.
4. Real-estate agents and developers who need buyer-facing 3D visualization.
5. Off-grid clients who need coordinated water, power, shade, food, and shelter planning.

### Secondary audiences

- natural builders and fabricators
- landscape and planting partners
- architects and engineers
- material suppliers
- nonprofits and community projects
- readers learning about better housing systems

### What visitors are buying

Visitors are not primarily buying drawings. They are buying:

- clarity before spending
- a property concept people can understand and desire
- coordination across many disconnected specialists
- fewer missed steps and fewer avoidable surprises
- a coherent aesthetic across architecture, landscape, utilities, and guest experience
- a story that helps an Airbnb, retreat, or property stand out

## 4. Offer architecture

The site must lead toward a paid first step rather than unlimited free consultation.

### Offer 1 — Site and concept audit

A bounded review of the land, listing, plan, climate, constraints, opportunity, and likely project path.

**Output**

- opportunity summary
- primary risks
- recommended build direction
- preliminary system map
- next-step scope

### Offer 2 — Biophilic concept package

A visual and strategic concept for one property or build.

**Output**

- spatial concept
- exterior and interior direction
- 3D massing or visualization
- material and planting direction
- water, shade, ventilation, and energy concept
- preliminary budget categories
- presentation package

### Offer 3 — Coordinated build package

Infinite Architecture coordinates the delivery team while qualified specialists perform regulated work.

**May include**

- local vendor and labor sourcing
- bid comparison
- schedule and dependency tracking
- procurement coordination
- design-intent reviews
- progress documentation
- landscape and living-system coordination
- guest-experience and listing presentation

Use a transparent fixed coordination fee, percentage management fee, or disclosed supplier margin. Do not imply undisclosed markups.

### Offer 4 — Real-estate 3D package

For agents, developers, and owners selling a future vision.

**Output**

- floor-plan interpretation
- biophilic redesign option
- still renders
- buyer-facing web presentation
- optional walkthrough or interactive 3D
- listing and campaign assets

### Offer 5 — Ongoing property evolution

Seasonal planting, guest-experience improvement, maintenance planning, resilience upgrades, and documentation.

## 6. Visual identity

### Core atmosphere

**Editorial biophilic luxury with material honesty.**

The public site should feel like:

- a Japanese architecture monograph
- a calm tropical design studio
- a cinematic real-estate presentation
- natural plaster, timber, water, shade, mist, and foliage
- generous white space and strong art direction
- curved forms against disciplined typography

It must not feel like:

- a construction supply catalog
- a crypto landing page
- a generic AI SaaS site
- a dark developer dashboard
- a nature-themed wellness template
- an unverified eco-claim brochure

### Two-mode system

#### Public editorial mode

Used for the homepage, studio, projects, services, climate atlas, and field notes.

- light mineral background
- black editorial typography
- large cinematic imagery
- calm, restrained motion
- asymmetric composition
- curved architecture as the visual signature

#### Technical field mode

Used for safety, diagrams, build-system details, internal dashboards, and hyper-roof engineering studies.

- deep charcoal background
- technical mono labels
- blueprint or telemetry accents
- high information density
- clear status, assumptions, and warnings

Do not alternate modes randomly within a page. A page must declare its mode.

## 7. Typography

The two official Infinite Architecture brand fonts are:

### Infinite Display — Bodoni Moda

Use for:

- hero words
- project titles
- large editorial section transitions
- numerals used as visual architecture
- short statements, never long paragraphs

Rules:

- weight: 400–600
- uppercase or carefully set title case
- line height: `0.82–0.96`
- letter spacing: `-0.045em` to `-0.015em`
- optical sizing enabled
- balance line breaks manually

### Infinite Editorial — Instrument Serif

Use for:

- subheads
- pull quotes
- article introductions
- project narratives
- captions that need warmth and humanity

Rules:

- weight: 400
- line height: `1.05–1.25` for large text
- line height: `1.45–1.65` for reading text
- use italics selectively

### Utility type

Use `Geist Sans`, `Manrope`, or a verified project sans for:

- navigation
- forms
- pricing
- buttons
- data
- accessibility-critical UI

Use `IBM Plex Mono` or `JetBrains Mono` for:

- measurements
- technical assumptions
- climate values
- diagram labels
- project status

The utility fonts support the brand; they are not headline substitutes.

### Scale

```css
--type-hero: clamp(4.25rem, 13vw, 12rem);
--type-display: clamp(3rem, 8vw, 7.5rem);
--type-section: clamp(2.25rem, 5vw, 5rem);
--type-lead: clamp(1.4rem, 2.4vw, 2.35rem);
--type-body-lg: clamp(1.05rem, 1.3vw, 1.25rem);
--type-body: 1rem;
--type-label: 0.72rem;
```

Use `text-wrap: balance` on headlines and `text-wrap: pretty` on body copy.

## 9. Imagery

### Image hierarchy

1. curved buildings integrated with land
2. luminous natural interiors
3. forest, water, slope, shade, and climate
4. close material detail
5. diagrams and process
6. people only when real and contextually useful

### Visual signatures

- shell-like roofs
- vaulted openings
- round windows
- layered landscape
- soft interior light
- fog, rain, water, leaf shadow, and filtered sun
- raw timber and mineral plaster
- architecture that appears grown, carved, folded, or sheltered

### Placeholder policy

Uploaded and reference-site images may be used only as internal design placeholders when rights are not established. Every placeholder requires an asset record with:

- source
- creator when known
- usage status
- license status
- replacement requirement
- final approval status

Do not publish third-party images as portfolio work.

### Generated-image standard

Generated architecture images must:

- use plausible structure and gravity
- avoid impossible glazing and unsupported spans
- maintain consistent openings and materials across views
- show climate-appropriate planting
- avoid luxury clichés unrelated to the concept
- be labeled as concept visualization
- never be represented as a completed built project

## 10. Layout system

### Public page geometry

- maximum content width: `1440px`
- reading width: `62ch–70ch`
- page gutter: `20px` mobile, `40px` tablet, `64px–96px` desktop
- section spacing: `96px` mobile, `144px–220px` desktop
- image radius: mostly `0–12px`; use true curved masks intentionally
- avoid equal three-card rows as the default pattern

### Preferred compositions

- asymmetric editorial grids
- one dominant image plus one or two supporting images
- full-bleed image with anchored typography
- sticky narrative with changing visual
- horizontal project gallery on desktop with native stacked fallback on mobile
- large negative space
- controlled overlap
- text aligned to architectural edges in the image

### Card rule

Do not put every piece of content in a card. Use spacing, rules, image crops, and typography first. Cards are reserved for:

- selectable build types
- project phases
- structured comparisons
- climate or material facts
- interactive tools

## 11. Motion system

Motion supports the architecture story; it does not become the story.

### Approved cinematic patterns

Use a maximum of three major motion systems on the homepage:

1. **Image-mask or curtain reveal** for the hero.
2. **Layered zoom parallax** for one architectural transition.
3. **Sticky narrative stack** for the service/process story.

Secondary patterns:

- SVG line draw for site and systems diagrams
- horizontal project gallery on desktop
- text mask reveal for one major phrase
- subtle image crossfade tied to section state

### Motion rules

- transform and opacity only for frequent motion
- avoid layout-thrashing properties
- isolate perpetual animation in memoized client components
- lazy-load GSAP and WebGL
- stop animation when offscreen
- provide reduced-motion equivalents
- no scroll hijacking on mobile
- no intro sequence that blocks access to the page
- motion must not delay the primary CTA
- target 60 fps on a mid-range mobile device

## 12. Mobile and Steve Krug rules

The mobile site must be clearer than the desktop site, not a compressed desktop composition.

- One idea per viewport.
- Primary navigation must be obvious.
- Touch targets minimum `44px`.
- Forms use native controls where possible.
- The user always knows where they are and what happens next.
- Avoid hidden horizontal scroll unless the interaction is clearly signaled.
- No autoplay audio.
- No essential information visible only on hover.
- Use `min-height: 100dvh`, never rigid `100vh`.
- Optimize the hero image for the mobile crop.
- Keep the first page interactive before all decorative assets finish loading.
- Every page ends with one relevant next action.

## 13. Content voice

### Voice

- calm
- direct
- informed
- practical
- visually literate
- honest about assumptions
- local when appropriate
- hopeful without making utopian claims

### Prefer

- `A roof can create shade, move air, collect water, and define the guest experience.`
- `We coordinate the concept, specialist team, and delivery path.`
- `This is a concept visualization. Structural design requires local engineering review.`

### Avoid

- `revolutionary`
- `game-changing`
- `eco-friendly` without evidence
- `sustainable` as an unsupported blanket claim
- `seamless`
- `next-generation`
- `we do everything`
- claims that a concept is permit-ready or structurally proven when it is not

### Naming categories

Use names that are both memorable and understandable:

- Roof as Lung
- Living Air
- Water Spine
- Shade Rooms
- Hyper Roofs
- Earth Interiors
- Garden Towers
- Tropical Vaults
- Off-Grid Core
- Guest Worlds

Each poetic name must have a plain-language explanation.

## 14. Comparison and evidence design

The site may compare conventional and regenerative approaches, but comparisons must expose assumptions.

Every chart or calculator must show:

- location
- time horizon
- included and excluded costs
- source date
- maintenance assumptions
- energy/water assumptions
- confidence level
- whether values are estimates or verified project data

Never publish fabricated precision. Use ranges when the evidence supports only ranges.

## 16. Accessibility and performance

### Accessibility

- semantic HTML
- skip link
- keyboard navigation
- visible focus states
- meaningful alt text
- captions or transcripts for video
- no text embedded only in images
- reduced-motion support
- contrast validation
- form errors linked to fields
- language attributes per locale

### Performance budgets

- mobile LCP target: under `2.5s`
- CLS target: under `0.1`
- interaction latency target: under `200ms`
- initial JavaScript: keep the public homepage lean; dynamically import WebGL and heavy motion
- responsive AVIF/WebP images
- no uncompressed video in the initial viewport
- poster image for every video

Performance claims must be verified in preview builds before release.

## 17. Localization

English is the primary source language.

Supported public locales:

- `en`
- `es-MX`
- `sw`
- `sr`

### Localization rules

- Mexican Spanish must be written naturally, not as literal machine translation.
- Swahili and Serbian require human review before prominent public release.
- Measurements, climate terminology, and currency must be localized.
- Brand and project names remain unchanged.
- Build-system safety warnings must not be published in a locale without review.
- Missing translations fall back to English and display an internal warning in development.

## 18. Technical implementation rules

- Preserve Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind, GSAP, and Framer Motion.
- Do not add a second animation framework.
- Confirm every dependency before import.
- Use server components by default.
- Isolate interactive sections as small client components.
- Dynamically import Three.js, globe, and Blender-viewer integrations.
- Avoid boolean-prop component sprawl; use explicit variants and composition.
- No dead links or `href="#"`.
- No placeholder copy in merged production code.
- Public and private layouts must remain separate.
- Every major feature must have a rollback path.
- Every branch must receive a Vercel preview before production merge.

---

## 19. Anti-patterns

Never ship:

- generic AI gradients
- dashboard styling on the public homepage
- excessive rounded cards
- a centered headline followed by three equal feature cards
- fake testimonials
- fake built projects
- unexplained ecological claims
- stock construction-worker imagery
- scroll effects that prevent reading
- seven competing animation patterns
- low-contrast serif body text
- public links to private operations
- engineering language without professional review
- hidden fees or misleading project roles
- unlicensed reference imagery

## 20. Awwwards quality gate

A page is not ready because it builds. It is ready only when it passes:

### User value

- the service is understandable in five seconds
- the primary audience recognizes itself
- the next action is obvious
- mobile users can complete the inquiry

### Visual quality

- typography is intentional
- imagery is coherent
- composition avoids template patterns
- motion has purpose
- no section feels unfinished

### Commercial quality

- each page supports a real offer
- project roles and boundaries are clear
- the site collects qualified inquiries
- proof and case-study status are honest

### Technical quality

- typecheck, lint, build, and tests pass
- preview deployment works
- key routes return successfully
- no critical accessibility failures
- no serious performance regression
- no exposed secrets

### Safety and sovereignty

- regulated work is assigned to qualified professionals
- concept imagery is labeled
- sources and assumptions are recorded
- owner retains control of domain, repository, hosting, data, and credentials
- rollback is documented

Recommended release threshold: **8.5/10 or higher**, with no critical safety, ownership, or reliability failure.
